\chapter{Loop-Transformation Kernels}
\label{ch:loop-kernels}
% Data: llr40Matrix/ (HPCAgent-Bench tag llr-focus40; v2 measured at benchmark commit 26a4f0cf).

The second kernel set isolates the loop transformations that the extracted kernels of
\Cref{ch:extracted} mix with everything else. It consists of 40 kernels of HPCAgent-Bench whose
efficient execution depends on dependence analysis and a loop transformation, such as
interchange, distribution or fusion, rather than on a library call. We refer to it as the
loop-transformation set, or LLR-40 after the benchmark's tag for it. This chapter describes
the kernels and the classes we group them into; \Cref{ch:evaluation} measures them.

\section{Kernel Set}
\label{sec:loop-kernel-set}

Twenty-nine of the 40 kernels come from TSVC-2~\cite{tsvc2}, the C version of the classic test
suite for vectorising compilers~\cite{callahan1988tsvc,maleki2011evaluation}: 26 keep their TSVC
names, and three are renamed variants (\texttt{argmax\_with\_index} is TSVC \texttt{s315},
\texttt{ext\_break\_capture} is \texttt{s332}, \texttt{ext\_war\_unit} is \texttt{s121}). The
remaining eleven were written for the benchmark and cover patterns TSVC lacks: producer and
consumer loops that must be fused through a temporary array, scans and ragged reductions, a
scatter with duplicate indices, stream compaction, a dependence whose distance is known only at
run time, and two wavefronts.

Every kernel is written once as a NumPy reference made of explicit loops, which is the
correctness oracle. From it, the benchmark's translators emit C, C++, Fortran and Numba versions.
For 35 of the 40 kernels the benchmark also carries a hand-written C reference ported from
TSVC-2, kept deliberately separate from the translated C so that the two can be compared. All
kernels compute in \texttt{float64}. At the problem size we measure (preset M), the translated C
version of a kernel takes between 39\,ms and 789\,ms per call, with a median of 100\,ms, which is
long enough that loop start-up and timer resolution do not matter.

\section{Transformation Classes}
\label{sec:loop-kernel-classes}

We group the kernels by the transformation they are meant to exercise
(\Cref{tab:loop-classes}). For 31 kernels the class is declared in the benchmark manifest; for
the other nine, whose manifests declare none, we derived it from the loop body. A hand check of
the nine derived labels against the kernel sources agrees with six, finds two disputable, and
disputes one. The two disputable ones are \texttt{ext\_break\_capture}, which the benchmark files
as a search loop in its TSVC original, and \texttt{ext\_war\_unit}, filed under induction
variables. The disputed one is \texttt{wf\_diff\_skew}, whose dependences are all carried by the
outer loop, so its inner loop is already parallel and needs no wavefront transformation. Two of
the declared labels are also questionable: \texttt{vpvts} and \texttt{vtvtv} are filed as control
flow but contain no branch. We keep all labels as recorded, so that the measurements remain
comparable with the benchmark's own, and point out where a label matters.

The classes are uneven. Reduction is the largest with ten kernels, and nine of the fourteen
classes have at most two. A per-class average over one or two kernels is a case study, not an
estimate, so the evaluation reports per-kernel results and uses the classes only to organise
them.
